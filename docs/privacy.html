Content delivery and visit statistics. Requests to keychange.dev pass through the network of Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend St., San Francisco, CA 94107, USA, which forwards them to GitHub Pages and delivers the response. Cloudflare processes the same request data listed above to deliver the site quickly and to protect it from attacks, and derives aggregate statistics from it: how many requests and visitors the site had, and from which countries. I only look at these totals and do not try to identify anyone. Nothing runs in your browser for this and nothing is stored on your device. Page-load measurement outside Europe. If your request reaches one of Cloudflare's data centres outside the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the UK, the page also loads Cloudflare Web Analytics, a script that measures how the page performed for you: load and paint timings, Core Web Vitals, which files the page loaded and how long each took, the page's memory use, its address and the referring page. It sends these to Cloudflare, which reports them to me only as totals. The script sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device and does not recognise you across visits; Cloudflare discards your IP address at the data centre. Requests handled by Cloudflare's data centres in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK never receive the script. The legal basis is Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in knowing how visitors find the site and how quickly it loads for them; the processor agreement and the Data Privacy Framework described above apply here as well. You can object at any time (Art. 21 GDPR), by email or simply by blocking the script with any content blocker.

No cookies, no tracking. This site sets no cookies and uses no tag managers and no advertising. Apart from the measurement script above, it embeds nothing from third parties: every stylesheet, image, animation and font used on these pages is served from keychange.dev itself or is a font already on your Mac.

Links out. The pages link to GitHub and Ko-fi. Those are ordinary links: nothing is sent to them until you click, at which point their own privacy policies apply.

The Keychange app

What it reads, and why it needs permission. Keychange has to know which physical keyboard produced a keystroke, which on macOS requires Input Monitoring permission. It reads which device an event came from and discards the rest. What you type is not recorded, not stored and not transmitted, and there is no logging of keystrokes anywhere in the app. You can verify this in the source.

What it stores. Your keyboard-to-layout mappings and your settings are saved locally on your Mac, in the app's own preferences. Nothing is synced and nothing leaves the machine. Deleting the app and its preferences removes them.

Update checks. If “Check for updates automatically” is switched on, the app asks GitHub for github.com/dennistimmermann/keychange/releases/latest/download/appcast.xml to see whether a newer release exists. That request transmits your IP address and the usual request headers, including the app version, to GitHub. It is the only network request the app makes, and you can turn it off in the settings. The legal basis is Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR, the legitimate interest in users being able to receive security and bug fixes; if you switch it off, no request is made.

No telemetry. There is no usage analytics, no crash reporting, no unique identifier, no account and no server of mine that the app talks to.

Donations

If you choose to donate, that happens on Ko-fi or GitHub Sponsors, on their websites and under their privacy policies. They are the controllers for whatever they collect. I receive only what their payout reporting shows me, which may include the name or handle you chose to display and any message you sent.
